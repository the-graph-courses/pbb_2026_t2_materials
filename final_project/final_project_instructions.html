Python Basics & Beyond Final Project: Statistics Dashboard with Quarto

Author

The GRAPH Courses

Overview

For your final project, you will build a multi-page Quarto dashboard that explores and visualizes real-world data, then give a short live presentation and defense of your work. This is an opportunity to consolidate your skills in data manipulation, visualization, and dashboard creation, while creating a portfolio-worthy project that you can share with future employers.

Checkpoint & Feedback Session (Mar 30)

Session 11 will be dedicated to getting feedback and mentorship on your project. Try to have at least a working draft of your dashboard by then so you can get the most out of the session.

Project Requirements

Data Selection

  • Choose at least two indicators from Gapminder’s data repository.
  • You can subset to a single year or include multiple years in your analysis.
  • If you would rather work with alternative datasets, perhaps those more relevant to your work, send an email to us at trainingteam@thegraphnetwork.org with your proposal. As long as your final dashboard meets the rubric requirements below, it could be a good alternative.

Dashboard Structure

Your dashboard should contain at least three pages, with at least the following:

  1. First Indicator Analysis
  2. Second Indicator Analysis
  3. Relationship between the two indicators. No statistical analysis is required; this can be purely descriptive/graphical.

Technical Requirements

  • The dashboard should include at least 12 display elements.
  • Elements include:
    • Value boxes
    • Interactive plots
    • Tables
    • Maps
    • Text boxes with descriptions of key insights
  • You should demonstrate your ability to use a range of pandas methods/functions. (Polars or Ibis are also acceptable.)
  • You should demonstrate your ability to create a range of plotly visualizations. (Matplotlib, plotnine, seaborn, etc. are also acceptable.)

Presentation & Defense (Apr 6)

On the final day, you will give a short live presentation of your dashboard (~5 minutes), followed by 1–2 questions from the instructor/TA about your code and design choices. The goal is to verify that you understand the code you wrote and can explain the decisions you made.

If you cannot attend the live session: email us at trainingteam@thegraphnetwork.org with a brief explanation of why you cannot join the presentation day. Then record a short video (for example with Loom or any screen-recording tool) that follows the same structure below—walk through your dashboard and cover the same points you would in person—and post the video link on the submission page next to your deployed dashboard link. After we review your video, we may schedule a short (~10 minute) Zoom with you if we need clarification about your code or design choices—this serves the same purpose as the in-session Q&A.

We recommend the following structure:

  1. Brief introduction — What data did you choose and why? (~1 minute)
  2. Live demo of your dashboard — Walk through the main pages, highlighting key visualizations and insights (~3 minutes)
  3. Wrap-up — Mention any limitations or things you would improve if you had more time (~1 minute)
  4. Q&A — The instructor/TA will ask 1–2 questions about your code or choices (~3 minutes)

Submission Instructions

  1. Upload your Quarto file (.qmd) to the submission page.
  2. Provide a link to the deployed dashboard on GitHub Pages.
  3. Present live during the session, or—if you cannot attend—follow the alternate steps in Presentation & Defense (email, recorded walkthrough on the submission page, possible brief Zoom).

Grading Rubric (100 points)

Data Selection and Manipulation (20 points)

  • Relevant data selected from Gapminder or approved alternative source
  • Data files are properly cleaned without errors or inconsistencies
  • Datasets are correctly merged when needed
  • Shows evidence of mastery of a range of pandas methods/functions

Dashboard Structure (15 points)

  • Contains required three pages (first indicator, second indicator, relationship)
  • Layout is logical, easy to navigate, and makes effective use of space
  • Design choices enhance understanding of the content

Visualization Elements (25 points)

  • Dashboard includes at least 12 display elements (value boxes, plots, tables, maps, etc.)
  • Plots have clear titles, labels, and appropriate scales
  • Interactive features (e.g., hover-over text) function correctly and display clear and useful information
  • Visual elements have titles or text boxes that explain the key insights
  • Appropriate choice of visualization types for the data

Code Quality (10 points)

  • Code is well-organized and readable
  • Code runs without errors
  • Efficient use of functions and operations
  • Consistent formatting and style

Deployment and Structure (10 points)

  • Dashboard is successfully deployed to GitHub Pages
  • Files are logically organized in repository
  • Repository contains only necessary files

Presentation & Defense (10 points)

  • Clear, well-organized walkthrough of the dashboard
  • Able to explain code logic and design choices when asked (live or in a brief follow-up Zoom, if scheduled)
  • Demonstrates genuine understanding of the tools and techniques used

Originality, Creativity, and Insight (10 points)

  • Original approaches to dashboard design, visualization, or interaction
  • Demonstrates thoughtful exploration of the data
  • Presents interesting patterns or relationships

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my dashboard doesn’t work perfectly?

That’s okay! Part of the presentation is discussing what didn’t work well and what you would improve. Honest reflection on limitations is valued.

Can I present a work-in-progress?

Ideally your dashboard should be complete, but it’s okay if some parts are still rough. Be clear about what’s preliminary and what your next steps would be.

How strict is the time limit?

We’ll aim for about 5 minutes for the presentation plus a couple of minutes for questions. Practice your timing to make sure you cover the key points.

What kind of questions will be asked?

The questions are designed to check that you understand your own code. For example: “Why did you choose this type of plot here?” or “How did you merge these two datasets?”

What if I miss the live presentation?

See Presentation & Defense for the alternate process (email with a brief reason, recorded walkthrough on the submission page, and possible short follow-up Zoom in place of live Q&A).
